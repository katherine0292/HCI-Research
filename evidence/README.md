## Counting and reporting decisions

- **Team study.** The [interview cluster summary](interview-clusters.csv) uses the final assignments in the master code register. The register and cluster workbook record the same 356 entries across 26 clusters. Theme-level participant coverage is calculated from distinct participant IDs within each theme. The findings summary lists 15 participants for the theme about items outside the routine. The final register contains entries from 14 distinct participants for that theme, which is the figure used in the case study. Counts document coverage in this dataset.
- **Field probe.** The [probe cluster summary](probe-clusters.csv) follows the affinity board. The incident count follows the numbered probe log in the research report. Code entries span four source types. An incident can appear in the log and the exit interview and give rise to multiple entries. The method notes account for 37 entries across four clusters. Those entries record factors such as recording burden, researcher prompts and reconstructed photos.
- **Portfolio brief.** The [research brief](../RESEARCH-BRIEF.md) was written retrospectively from the study instruments, design rationale and report. It describes the academic project and maps workstreams to completed portfolio artefacts.

## Selected references

- Overdevest, N., Patibanda, R., Saini, A., van den Hoven, E., & Mueller, F. F. (2023). *Towards Designing for Everyday Embodied Remembering*. The diary study informed the project's focus on physical routines and everyday remembering. [DIS paper](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/175724)
- Altmann, E. M., & Trafton, J. G. (2002). *Memory for Goals*. The model provides a framework for thinking about retrieval cues and returning to a suspended task. [Cognitive Science article](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog2601_2)
- Dismukes, R. K. (2012). *Prospective Memory in Workplace and Everyday Situations*. The review situates intended actions in everyday and workplace settings. [Current Directions in Psychological Science article](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721412447621)
- Boehner, K., Vertesi, J., Sengers, P., & Dourish, P. (2007). *How HCI Interprets the Probes*. The paper provides context for interpreting what probes produce. [CHI paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/1240624.1240789)
- Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2006, 2019). Thematic analysis informed the group synthesis and the reflexive analysis of the probe. [Using Thematic Analysis in Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1191/1478088706qp063oa) · [Reflecting on Reflexive Thematic Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1080/2159676X.2019.1628806)
