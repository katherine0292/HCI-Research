# Research instruments

The guides below are edited English versions of the study instruments and omit participant-specific details.

## Team interview guide

Use a semi-structured conversation. Start with a recent, concrete departure and follow the participant's account through what they carried, where they put it, how they checked and what made them notice a problem.

| Topic | Core prompts |
| --- | --- |
| Everyday belongings | What do you normally take with you? What is always with you? What changes with your destination or purpose? |
| Leaving a place | Walk me through the last time you left a shared space. What did you do just before you left? How did you know you were ready? |
| Bodily routines | What do your hands do during the check? Do weight, shape, sound or the feel of a pocket tell you anything? |
| Environmental cues | Where do things normally go in that setting? What do you see, hear or touch that brings an item to mind? |
| Breakdown and recovery | Tell me about a recent time you forgot or nearly forgot something. What was happening just before you left? What first made you realise? What happened next? |

Useful follow-ups include where the item was, whether the participant was holding something else, and what was different from a typical departure. Ask for an example where the routine worked under similar conditions. This comparison helps identify what actually changed.

## Field probe protocol

### Introductory interview and handoff

1. Explain the study, voluntary participation and available recording options. Ask permission before recording the interview.
2. Ask the participant to walk through preparation for a typical visit, including how she selects keys and documents and which check comes last.
3. Ask for a recent near miss. Reconstruct the sequence from preparation through interruption and recovery.
4. Draw a Spot Map together for the car, desk and property entrance. Mark the normal homes of keys, papers and phone, plus any check tied to a movement or threshold.
5. Introduce the kit and ask what fits the workday. Keep participation separate from the participant's work priorities and adapt the materials to her preferences.

### Probe kit

| Element | Participant prompt | Research purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Snap Photo** | When it is safe and convenient, photograph where you put the keys, papers or phone if the spot is unusual. Skip the photo if it could show a client, address, access detail or document content. | Show the relationship between the object's usual home and the temporary location. |
| **WeChat voice note** | When you have a quiet moment, send a short note about what you were doing, what interrupted you, what you first noticed and what happened next. The target was about 10–20 seconds. | Capture the sequence in the participant's words, including procedural slips with little to photograph. |
| **Quick-reaction sticker** | If there is little time to explain, choose a reaction such as “a bit annoying,” “no big deal” or “startled.” You can add context later if you want. | Provide a low-effort record and a participant-defined sense of the incident's significance. |
| **Reminder eraser** | Keep the eraser in a convenient place. Its prompt is a reminder to record something relevant when you have time. | Cue participation without requiring a change to a tool she uses for work. The planned keyring tag was replaced at handoff. |
| **Spot Map** | Sketch the places you move through and mark where the items normally go. We can draw it together. | Establish a baseline for interpreting later reports about changed placement and location-based checks. |

### Exit interview

Review the kit records with the participant. For each incident, ask what was underway, where the interruption landed, what the participant did next and what first made the problem apparent. Ask whether a photo shows the original scene or a later reconstruction. Compare the event with the Spot Map and establish which details are approximate. End by asking what went unrecorded, which tools fit the workday and how taking part changed the participant's attention or behaviour.

## Participant safeguards

The study materials describe voluntary participation, the option to skip prompts, permission to record interviews and confidentiality. The introductory interview discussed keeping client and property details out of probe records. The portfolio uses aggregates and paraphrases, with original records retained separately from the repository.

The probe design report also explored an ethics adaptation for potential future work with Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander participants. It was preparatory work for a separate context. Any future study would involve community-led consultation on participation, governance and data use. See the [AIATSIS Code of Ethics](https://aiatsis.gov.au/research/ethical-research).
