# Research brief

**Remembering in Motion**

**Prepared by** [@katherine0292](https://github.com/katherine0292)

**Project type** Personal qualitative HCI research project in an academic setting

I wrote this retrospective brief to capture the scope of the completed research and connect each objective to a portfolio artefact.

## Research problem

When people leave a place, they often rely on familiar routines. A bag holds the essentials, a pocket pat confirms that a key is present, or a final look catches something left on a table. These checks work within a particular arrangement of objects, places and movements. A call, a conversation or a change in where something is placed can disrupt that arrangement. An omission may become visible only when the item is needed or someone asks about an unfinished task.

The project examines how people build those routines, what happens when a routine is disrupted, and which cues help them recover. The design question is whether an interaction or physical cue can help someone identify the next unfinished action during normal work.

## Research questions

1. **Everyday routines.** How do people use containers, fixed locations, movements and sensory cues to remember belongings when leaving a place?
2. **Breakdown and recovery.** When a call, conversation or competing task displaces a check, what changes in the sequence, what first signals a problem, and how does the person recover?
3. **Participant burden.** Which reporting methods fit short gaps in a mobile workday? Which parts of an incident are captured close to the event and which depend on later reconstruction?
4. **Design opportunities.** Which cues could help someone find an item moved temporarily, resume an interrupted check, or verify that a final action is complete?

## Participants and settings

The interview phase examined accounts from 15 adults about everyday transitions across home, work and shared spaces. For the field phase, I focused on one property manager who regularly carried keys and documents between an office, a car and property entrances. She was recruited by convenience sampling and was separate from the interview sample. Calls, conversations and overlapping work tasks made her routine an information-rich setting for examining interruption and recovery.

## Review criteria

- A reviewer can trace the field question and kit choices to the interview findings.
- The analysis identifies what interrupted the routine, what the participant did next and what eventually signalled the problem.
- Counts have a defined source and unit, and the write-up accounts for delayed reports, recreated scenes and researcher influence.
- Each design hypothesis has an evidence trail and a proposed way to evaluate it. The completed scope is qualitative research and synthesis.
